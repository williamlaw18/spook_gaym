# Sacrifice System

Status: draft

## Summary

The modifier layer of monster creation (see `monster-creation.md`) is fed
by a dedicated sacrifice sub-chain, separate from ordinary resource
gathering:

1. **Capture** — the player captures a creature (from the caverns, or
   possibly one of their own released monsters) rather than harvesting a
   material.
2. **Sacrifice** — the captured creature is fed into a dedicated
   device/ritual in the factory/homebase, distinct from the base and
   characteristics crafting stations.
3. **Convert** — the ritual converts the sacrificed creature into a
   modifier resource, which can then be layered onto a monster's base +
   characteristics during creation.

This is deliberately framed as a darker, more consequential act than
regular resource collection — it's the game leaning into "blood sacrifice"
imagery, and should feel weightier than gathering ore/soul material.

## Design notes

- Capture needs its own mechanic distinct from combat/harvesting — likely a
  non-lethal capture tool/method, since the creature needs to arrive alive
  to be sacrificed deliberately by the player rather than killed
  incidentally.
- What can be captured (only native cavern creatures? only the player's own
  released monsters? both?) has real fictional and difficulty implications:
  sacrificing your own creations ties back into Core Tenet 1 (creation is
  the difficulty curve) in an even more direct, costly way.
- Modifier potency/darkness should likely scale with what's sacrificed (a
  minor cavern critter vs. a powerful player-made monster), giving the
  player a risk/reward lever independent of the base/characteristics layers.
- This system is a strong candidate for pushing corruption forward — see
  `demon-and-corruption.md` — since it's the most overtly demonic/ritual
  act in the game's crafting loop.

## Open questions

- Capture mechanic specifics (tool, trap, non-lethal takedown, etc.).
- Whether sacrifice has a direct fictional cost (e.g., contributes to
  corruption directly, angers/pleases the demon on its own).
- Whether captured creatures can be kept/used for anything other than
  sacrifice (temporary allies, other side systems).
- Resource/time cost of the ritual itself, and whether it can fail.
